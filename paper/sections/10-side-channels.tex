\question{Does the system disclose per-practice information outside the model path?}
\label{sec:sidechannels}
\statusMethod

\begin{shortanswer}
It did, in two places, and both are now closed by default. The per-round logs printed each
practice's identifier alongside its cohort size and performance --- a disclosure to whoever reads
the SuperLink logs, entirely independent of the gradients. And the practice data export carried a
direct patient identifier that nothing downstream used. Anonymised logging with a cohort-size floor
is now the deployment default, and the identifier is gone from the export.
\end{shortanswer}

\subsection{The metric logging channel}

The project reports metrics at two levels by design: a global sample-weighted mean, and the
\emph{worst} individual practice. The second exists because a federation can look strong on average
while collapsing on one outlier practice, and it is also the fairness measure for T2.5. It is a
requirement, not an accident.

But the per-practice detail lines named the practice, its cohort size, its AUROC and its
sensitivity, every round. For a small practice that combination is itself disclosive to whoever
operates or reads the logs --- and it does not depend on any gradient. It was a genuine channel and
it was open by default because it made research output easier to read.

Two controls exist and are now \textbf{on by default} in the deployment configuration:
anonymised reporting, which drops the identity while keeping the worst-practice figure that the
requirement exists to protect; and a cohort-size floor below which a practice's line is suppressed
entirely. The research runners keep the identified view behind their own explicit flags, so baseline
numbers remain interpretable without weakening the deployment default.

\subsection{The direct identifier in the export}

The canonical extraction query selected a patient identifier into every practice's CSV. Nothing
downstream consumed it --- the loader selects only the feature columns, so it never reached a model
--- but the file on the practice's disk carried it, which made that file personal data rather than a
pseudonymous extract, and pulled it into the scope of every access-control and retention obligation
attaching to such data.

\textbf{The column has been removed.} If a stable join key across extractions is genuinely needed
later, the replacement is a per-practice salted hash adopted deliberately, not the raw identifier
restored --- and a hashed identifier remains pseudonymous data, not anonymous.

\paragraph{One residual channel, documented rather than silently left.} The export is still ordered
by that identifier. The column is gone, but \emph{row order still follows it}. If identifiers are
assigned chronologically, row position is a weak signal for relative enrolment order. Downstream code
must not treat row position as information. Randomising the order removes the channel at the cost of
reproducible extracts; we have documented the trade-off in the query rather than deciding it
unilaterally, because reproducibility of extractions may matter to the collection protocol.
